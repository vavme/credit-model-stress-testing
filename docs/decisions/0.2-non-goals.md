# 0.2 Non-goals List

**Phase:** 0 (Scope Lock & Setup)
**Tag:** [You]
**Status:** Done
**Decided:** 2026-09-13

## The decision

Eleven explicit non-goals, pinned in `README.md` under **Non-goals**:

1. Not competing for Kaggle leaderboard AUC: success is honest offline evaluation on our own held-out split, not a public score (`application_test.csv` is unlabeled and out of scope entirely).
2. Not using all 6 auxiliary tables: sticking to `application_train.csv` and a 25-40 feature shortlist from it.
3. Not running a hyperparameter tuning marathon: a reasonably-tuned GBM is enough; the contribution is the corruption/complexity trade-off, not marginal AUC.
4. Not measuring or optimizing training time, compute, or infrastructure cost: at ~300K rows, logistic regression and a GBM both train in under a minute. Not a meaningful axis at this scale.
5. Not using a neural network as the complex model, and not testing data-hunger/sample-efficiency: the complex model is a GBM (XGBoost).
6. Not quantifying real implementation, governance/validation, or monitoring cost in dollars or hours: these are named as motivation for why complexity isn't free, not something this project measures directly.
7. Not optimizing against a specific bank's business objective (a target approval rate or bad rate cutoff): the comparison is judged on overall discrimination (AUC/Gini/KS).
8. Not declaring a fixed accuracy threshold (e.g. "must beat by 5 points") as the definition of "worth it": materiality is read off the bootstrapped confidence interval on the complexity premium at each corruption severity level.
9. Not building a production-serving or real-time scoring pipeline: this stays a research/offline comparison, not a deployable service.
10. Not running a formal fairness or disparate-impact audit: a real bank deployment would need this, but it's a distinct project on its own and out of scope here.
11. Not benchmarking other complex-model families (LightGBM, CatBoost, etc.): XGBoost is the one complex model, fixed by design, not chosen through a bake-off.

## How it got here

Items 1-8 came out of an earlier working session: talking through where a project like this typically scope-creeps (chasing leaderboard AUC, tuning marathons, pulling in every auxiliary table) and turning that into an explicit list, including the two calls that mattered most, fixing XGBoost as the one complex model (so compute/data-hunger never becomes a variable) and defining "worth it" via a bootstrapped CI rather than an arbitrary accuracy threshold.

Items 9-11 were added in a second pass, after the first eight were already locked. They cover a different kind of scope creep, not "grinding for marginal AUC" but "quietly expanding into an adjacent, bigger project" (production deployment, a fairness audit, a multi-model bake-off). All three are real, legitimate concerns for an actual bank deployment; naming them as out-of-scope here is a deliberate boundary, not an oversight.
