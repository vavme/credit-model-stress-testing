Credit Risk Under Data Quality Degradation

This project asks when a complex credit model stops beating a simple scorecard as data quality gets worse. This site shows the analysis that comes first: what is in the data, what is missing, and what could go wrong.

Five messages

  1. The data covers granted loans, and TARGET flags early payment trouble. It does not mean final loss, and rejected applicants are not in the table. Read why
  2. Only {{f:default_rate_pct}} of loans default, so accuracy would mislead. An approve-everyone rule scores {{f:acc_all_zero_pct}} and finds no defaulter. See the balance
  3. About a quarter of the feature cells are empty, and the gaps are not random. {{f:n_cols_gt50}} of {{f:n_cols}} columns are more than half empty, and only {{f:complete_rows_pct}} of rows are complete. See the gaps
  4. Three external scores carry most of the signal. Their lowest and highest deciles differ by {{f:ext_range_min_pp}} to {{f:ext_range_max_pp}} points of default rate. Most other columns are weak alone. See the signal
  5. The study design is set before any experiment. {{f:n_shortlist}} of {{f:n_cols}} columns are proposed, and five corruptions are mapped to three real-world failure types, with draft predictions on record. See the plan

Section 1 of 9

Each row is one granted loan, and TARGET flags early payment trouble, not final loss

  • Home Credit is a consumer lender. The Kaggle contest asked teams to predict which applicants would have repayment problems. {{tag:general}}{{tag:nv}}
  • One row is one loan. The ID column names the loan, not the person. TARGET is 1 when the client paid late by more than X days on at least one of the first Y instalments. The file does not say what X and Y are. {{tag:doc}}
  • Every row has an outcome, so every row is a loan that was granted. Rejected applicants are not in the table. This is inferred, not verified. {{tag:general}}{{tag:nv}} There is also no calendar date. That is on purpose: it lets the experiment change one thing at a time, the damage we add. {{tag:doc}}
  • Banks use this kind of data to build an {{term:scorecard|application scorecard}}: a score for the chance of default, used to approve, price or review a loan. {{tag:general}}
So what for this project. Our results describe approved loans in this sample. They do not describe every applicant, and they do not describe final losses.

Section 3 of 9

Only {{f:default_rate_pct}} of loans default, so accuracy is a misleading way to judge a model

  • {{f:n_pos}} loans have payment trouble and {{f:n_neg}} do not. That is {{f:imbalance_ratio}} loans without trouble for every one with trouble. {{tag:data}}
  • A rule that approves everyone is {{f:acc_all_zero_pct}} accurate and finds none of the {{f:n_pos}} problem loans. {{tag:data}}
  • So we judge models by how well they rank risk, with {{term:auc|AUC}}, {{term:gini|Gini}}, {{term:ks|KS}} and {{term:prauc|PR-AUC}}. They do not depend on one cutoff. {{tag:general}}
  • A useless score gets an AUC of {{f:auc_useless}}. Its PR-AUC equals the default rate, {{f:ap_floor}}. {{tag:data}}
So what for this project. Every later result is judged by rank measures. When we hide defaults in the labels later, the class ratio changes too: hiding half of the defaults would cut the default rate to {{f:hide_50_rate_pct}}.

Section 4 of 9

Missing data is widespread, comes in blocks, and is linked to default, so how we handle it matters

  • {{f:n_cols_any_missing}} of {{f:n_cols}} columns have missing values. {{f:n_cols_gt50}} are more than half empty. Only {{f:complete_rows_pct}} of rows are complete. {{tag:data}}
  • The missing values form {{f:n_patterns}} patterns. The {{f:n_building_patterns}} building patterns move together: their correlation is between {{f:phi_building_min}} and {{f:phi_building_max}}. {{tag:data}}
  • Some gaps are by design. Car age is missing for {{f:own_car_age_missing_nonowner_pct}} of clients without a car. Occupation is missing for {{f:occupation_missing_pensioner_pct}} of pensioners. {{tag:data}}
  • In {{f:n_patterns_positive_gap}} of the {{f:n_patterns_gt5}} patterns that are more than {{f:thr_missing_pct}} missing, rows with a missing value default more often than rows with a value, by {{f:gap_min_pp}} to {{f:gap_max_pp}} points. Occupation goes the other way, mostly because of the pensioner effect. {{tag:data}}
Missing at random or not? Three terms in plain words

{{term:mcar|MCAR}}, missing completely at random. A value is missing for no reason at all. Nothing in this data looks like that.

{{term:mar|MAR}}, missing at random. Whether a value is missing depends on other columns we can see. Car age is missing exactly when the client has no car.

{{term:mnar|MNAR}}, missing not at random. Whether a value is missing depends on the value itself, or on something we cannot see. We cannot prove this from the data, because the value we would need to check is the one that is missing.

Our reading of the patterns below is a hypothesis. {{tag:hyp}}

So what for this project. Scenario A starts from data that is already missing. A damage level of 0% will mean the natural level, not none.

Section 5 of 9

Three external scores carry most of the signal, and most other columns are weak on their own

  • For the three EXT_SOURCE columns, the default rate differs by {{f:ext_range_min_pp}} to {{f:ext_range_max_pp}} points between the lowest and highest {{term:decile|decile}}. For EXT_SOURCE_3 it falls from {{f:ext_source_3_dec_first}} to {{f:ext_source_3_dec_last}}. {{tag:data}}
  • The next strongest are goods price ({{f:goods_range_pp}} points, but not a straight line), then employment length ({{f:dayemp_range_pp}}) and age ({{f:birth_range_pp}}). {{tag:data}}
  • Only {{f:n_corr_gt010}} of {{f:n_numeric_corr}} numeric features have a correlation above {{f:corr_threshold}} with default. {{f:n_noise}} of {{f:n_ranked}} look like noise next to a test with shuffled labels. {{tag:data}}
  • One correlation number can hide the shape. Annuity amount has a {{term:spearman|Spearman}} correlation of {{f:annuity_spearman}}, yet its default rate goes from {{f:annuity_dec_first}} up to {{f:annuity_dec_peak}} and back down to {{f:annuity_dec_last}}. {{tag:data}}
So what for this project. Models will lean on three scores whose source and timing are not documented. If they hold information from after the loan was granted, every result is inflated. That is a hypothesis. {{tag:hyp}} These scores are also the most important place to test damage.

  • One income is {{f:income_max}} while the median is {{f:income_median}}. {{tag:data}}
  • {{f:n_near_constant}} columns are at least {{f:thr_const_pct}} one value. {{f:n_building_stems}} building columns come in three near-identical versions, with correlation {{f:building_twin_corr_min}} or more. {{tag:data}}

Five candidate corruptions are mapped to three failure types, with draft predictions written before any experiment

What the draft predicts. Complexity stops paying only when the hidden defaults depend on a feature. For the four feature-level cases, the gain from the complex model stays above zero. {{tag:hyp}}

Where the two differ. The hypothesis expects the gap to shrink gradually under missing data and label lag, and to drop sharply under scale or schema drift. The draft does not expect a sharp drop under drift. The experiment will show which view is closer. {{tag:hyp}}
