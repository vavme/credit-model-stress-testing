Frequently asked questions

Prepared reader questions. They were written in advance by the author's assistant, not collected from real readers. Each answer uses only numbers from the analysis.

Why not use the test file from Kaggle?

That file has no outcome labels. It exists for the contest leaderboard, so it cannot tell us how well a model works. We cut our own train, validation and test split from the labelled file instead: {{f:train_share_pct}}, {{f:val_share_pct}} and {{f:test_share_pct}} of the {{f:n_raw}} loans, with seed {{f:seed}}. The test part stays sealed until the final evaluation. {{tag:doc}}{{tag:data}}

Why does the data have no dates, and is that a problem?

The table only has offsets, such as the number of days before the application. {{tag:data}} That is a limit: we cannot test how a model holds up over real time. It is also a benefit. With no calendar, no economic cycle can explain a result, so the damage we add is the only thing that changes. {{tag:doc}}

Does TARGET = 1 mean the client never repaid?

No. It means a payment was late by more than X days in at least one of the first Y instalments. A client can pay late once, then pay in full, and still be a one. The file does not say what X and Y are. {{tag:doc}} Of {{f:n_train}} loans in the train split, {{f:n_pos}} are a one, which is {{f:default_rate_pct}}. {{tag:data}}

Why is a high accuracy not a good result here?

Because {{f:acc_all_zero_pct}} is what a rule gets by approving everyone and flagging no one. That rule finds none of the {{f:n_pos}} loans with trouble. So we judge models by how well they rank risk, using {{term:auc|AUC}}, {{term:gini|Gini}}, {{term:ks|KS}} and {{term:prauc|PR-AUC}}. {{tag:data}}

Is the missing data random?

Not in a simple way. In {{f:n_patterns_positive_gap}} of the {{f:n_patterns_gt5}} patterns that are more than {{f:thr_missing_pct}} missing, rows with a missing value default more often, by {{f:gap_min_pp}} to {{f:gap_max_pp}} points. Some gaps are by design: car age is missing for {{f:own_car_age_missing_nonowner_pct}} of clients without a car. {{tag:data}} We cannot prove whether values are missing because of their own value, which would be {{term:mnar|MNAR}}. Our reading is a hypothesis. {{tag:hyp}}

Which column matters most, and why not trust one correlation number?

The three EXT_SOURCE scores. Their default rate differs by {{f:ext_range_min_pp}} to {{f:ext_range_max_pp}} points between the lowest and highest decile, and EXT_SOURCE_3 has a Pearson correlation of {{f:ext_source_3_pearson}} with default. {{tag:data}} But one number can hide the shape. Annuity amount has a Spearman correlation of {{f:annuity_spearman}}, while its default rate goes from {{f:annuity_dec_first}} up to {{f:annuity_dec_peak}} and back down to {{f:annuity_dec_last}}. {{tag:data}}

Why do gender and age appear on this site?

They are columns in the data, so they are shown as descriptive features. This project does not use them to make decisions about people, and a fairness review is out of scope for this phase. {{tag:doc}} Age has a Pearson correlation of {{f:days_birth_pearson}} with default, so dropping it would cost some signal. That trade-off is the author's call. {{tag:data}}

Are rejected applicants in the data?

As far as we can tell, no. Every row has an outcome, and only a granted loan has one. This is inferred, not verified. {{tag:general}}{{tag:nv}} A model built on this data ranks people who already passed the lender's screen.
